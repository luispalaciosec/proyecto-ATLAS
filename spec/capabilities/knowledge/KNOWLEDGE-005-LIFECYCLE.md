# Knowledge Lifecycle

---

# Purpose

This document defines how organizational knowledge evolves throughout its entire lifecycle inside Atlas.

Knowledge is not static.

It is continuously created, validated, improved, executed, observed and refined.

The lifecycle described in this document is independent of implementation technologies.

It defines the conceptual evolution of organizational intelligence.

Every Atlas capability participates in different stages of this lifecycle.

---

# Why a Lifecycle?

Knowledge changes.

Organizations change.

People learn.

Processes evolve.

Policies are updated.

Products improve.

Customer expectations shift.

Atlas does not preserve static information.

Atlas preserves the continuous evolution of organizational intelligence.

The lifecycle provides governance, traceability and consistency throughout that evolution.

---

# Lifecycle Principles

The Knowledge Lifecycle follows several permanent principles.

## Knowledge is never finished.

Every version represents the best organizational understanding available at a specific point in time.

---

## Knowledge is cumulative.

New knowledge enriches previous understanding.

Atlas preserves historical context instead of replacing it.

---

## Knowledge is observable.

Knowledge should produce measurable outcomes.

Execution generates observations.

Observations improve knowledge.

---

## Knowledge is governed.

Every lifecycle transition requires organizational responsibility.

Atlas never assumes anonymous ownership.

---

## Knowledge is auditable.

Every state transition becomes part of organizational history.

Nothing meaningful disappears.

↓

Versioned

↓

The lifecycle is intentionally independent from implementation details.

Organizations may introduce additional intermediate states.

---

# Stage 1 — Idea

Knowledge begins as an observation.

Ideas may originate from:

People

Meetings

Projects

Metrics

Incidents

Research

Customer feedback

Artificial Intelligence

Ideas are not yet organizational knowledge.

They represent potential knowledge.

---

# Stage 2 — Draft

Ideas become explicit organizational statements.

Draft knowledge is:

structured

owned

versioned

traceable

Drafts remain private until reviewed.

Drafts may evolve rapidly.

---

# Stage 3 — Review

Draft knowledge enters organizational validation.

Review may involve:

subject matter experts

stakeholders

legal review

technical validation

quality assurance

Review evaluates correctness rather than popularity.

Knowledge is strengthened through critical evaluation.

---

# Stage 4 — Approved

Approved knowledge becomes official organizational knowledge.

Approval establishes:

ownership

trust

governance

organizational acceptance

Approved knowledge may now influence organizational decisions.

Approval does not imply permanent correctness.

---

# Stage 5 — Operational

Operational knowledge becomes available for organizational use.

At this stage it may be consumed by:

Compiler

Runtime

Workflow

Retrieval

Agents

Dashboards

Human users

Operational knowledge actively influences organizational behavior.

---

# Stage 6 — Observed

Execution produces observations.

Examples include:

runtime metrics

user feedback

execution logs

exceptions

performance indicators

business outcomes

Observations become new evidence.

Evidence influences organizational trust.

---

# Stage 7 — Improved

Knowledge evolves through continuous learning.

Improvements may include:

clarifications

new evidence

corrected assumptions

extended scope

updated governance

Knowledge grows through organizational experience.

Improvement never destroys historical understanding.

---

# Stage 8 — Versioned

Significant improvements create new versions.

Versioning preserves:

historical decisions

organizational context

previous assumptions

execution history

Each version represents a snapshot of organizational intelligence.

Identity remains constant across versions.

---

# Stage 9 — Retired

Knowledge eventually becomes obsolete.

Reasons include:

technology replacement

organizational restructuring

regulatory changes

product discontinuation

strategic decisions

Retired knowledge is no longer active.

However, it remains historically relevant.

---

# Stage 10 — Archived

Archived knowledge becomes part of permanent organizational memory.

Archived knowledge:

cannot be executed

cannot influence Runtime

remains searchable

remains traceable

supports historical analysis

Atlas never permanently deletes organizational intelligence.

---

# Lifecycle Transitions

Transitions between states are governed.

Typical transitions include:

Idea → Draft

Draft → Review

Review → Approved

Approved → Operational

Operational → Observed

Observed → Improved

Improved → Versioned

Versioned → Operational

Operational → Retired

Retired → Archived

Organizations may define additional transitions provided semantic consistency is preserved.

---

# Capability Responsibilities

Different Atlas capabilities participate in different stages.

Knowledge

Creates and structures organizational knowledge.

Compiler

Validates operational knowledge before execution.

Runtime

Produces execution observations.

Memory

Preserves lifecycle evolution.

Retrieval

Discovers active and historical knowledge.

Workflow

Coordinates organizational review and approval processes.

Agents

Generate recommendations based on operational and historical knowledge.

Each capability contributes to knowledge evolution.

None owns the complete lifecycle independently.

---

# Lifecycle Invariants

Every Knowledge Object follows several mandatory invariants.

Identity never changes.

History is immutable.

Version history is preserved.

Ownership is always explicit.

Operational knowledge must originate from approved knowledge.

Archived knowledge remains discoverable.

Trust evolves continuously.

Observations never replace historical evidence.

These invariants ensure long-term consistency.
